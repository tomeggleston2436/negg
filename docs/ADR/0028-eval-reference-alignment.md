# 0028: eval registers hand-cropped references and describes tone

## Status

Accepted, 2026-10-24.

## Context

`negg eval` scored a reference render by pixel-wise CIEDE2000, at the render's size or on a shared analysis grid when the two had the same framing at another size (Ruling 32). That assumes the reference tool exported the same framing. Negative Lab Pro (NLP) exports do not: they are 8-bit sRGB JPEGs cropped by hand, at their own size, sometimes mirrored relative to the RAW (film scanned base side to the lens) and possibly turned. The frames those exports belong to also need a per-frame crop to remove the film holder (`Settings.geometry`, Ruling 70), which the corpus manifest could not express. And a single CIEDE2000 mean counts a difference without saying whether it is contrast, highlight roll-off, saturation or cast.

## Decision

Add tool `nlp` and an optional per-frame `geometry` to the manifest, rendered as a frame override in roll mode; register every NLP reference (and any NegPy or negadoctor reference that matches neither the render's size nor its analysis grid) onto the render by an 8-orientation, uniform-scale, translation and small-rotation search on L* and its edges, score only the overlap, and report tone and colour descriptors beside CIEDE2000.

## Consequences

- Manifest: `references[].tool` accepts `"nlp"`; `[[frame]]` takes `geometry` with the fields, defaults and validation of `Settings.geometry` (unknown keys rejected as everywhere in the manifest). The eval renders such a frame with `resolve(roll, FrameOverrides { geometry })`, so it still pools with its roll, measures the grey card on the uncropped render (the card rect stays on the whole scan), then applies the geometry (step 8) before scoring references. Frames without it render exactly as before.
- Registration (`crates/negg-cli/src/eval/align.rs`): luminance pyramids by box averaging (render linear Rec.2020, reference encoded sRGB, each block then converted to L*), finest level at most 1536 pixels on the long side, coarsest at most 48. On the coarsest level an exhaustive search over the 8 orientations (quarter turn, then mirror, as `Geometry` orders them), scales at which the reference width covers 0.6 to 1.1 of the render width in steps of one coarse pixel at the far edge, and every whole coarse-pixel translation within 5% of the frame. A placement counts only if the overlap covers at least 30% of the render and 75% of the reference and the reference is at most 1.1 of the render in either direction; without the last two bounds a blown-up corner of an unrelated picture fitted smooth synthetic scenes at an L* correlation over 0.6. The best placement per orientation is refined level by level with a coordinate hill climb on scale and offsets (down to a quarter level pixel, an eighth on the finest level), keeping four candidates after the coarsest level and two after each finer one.
- Why 0.6 to 1.1: a hand crop rarely removes more than a fifth of the width on each side, and the export may keep a sliver of border the render's own crop removed. Uniform scale and quarter turns only, because neither tool resamples one axis alone or rotates freely. Controller choice, TODO(measure) on the first real NLP references.
- Verdict: the search maximises the normalised correlation of L*, but the reported `correlation` is that of the L* gradient magnitude at the best placement on the finest level. Shared smooth shading gives unrelated synthetic scenes an L* correlation of 0.6 or more; their edges correlate at about 0.07 to 0.22, against 0.9 to 0.99 for the same scene (exported smaller, mirrored, turned, quantised to 8 bits). A reference below `MIN_CORRELATION = 0.5` is `unaligned`: listed under `skipped` with its best placement under the frame's `unaligned`, and left out of every mean. Controller choice, TODO(measure). A NegPy or negadoctor reference that fails to register stays a size mismatch, exactly as before.
- Scoring: the reference is resampled bilinearly at the point each render pixel centre sees, over the overlap rectangle only, through the existing CIEDE2000 statistics. Per registered reference the report adds `alignment` (turn, flip, scale in render pixels per reference pixel, offset of the oriented reference's top-left pixel centre, overlap fraction of the render, edge and L* correlations) and `tone`: L* at the 1st, 50th and 99th percentiles (histogram bins of 0.01), mean C*ab, mean a* and b* for render and reference, and the mean a* and b* difference. The summary adds `de2000_mean_by_tool.nlp` and `tone` (signed and absolute means of the differences, overall and per tool); NLP references count in `de2000_mean` and `de2000_p95_mean` like any other. `eval.toml` gains `tone_l_abs_diff`, `tone_chroma_abs_diff` and `tone_cast_ab` (placeholders 0.5, defaults when absent), compared only when a report has tone means.
- Schema: every new report field is optional and omitted from the JSON when empty, so a corpus without NLP references or geometry writes byte-identical results, older reports load as baselines, and `REPORT_SCHEMA_VERSION` stays 2. The committed `eval-baseline.json` is unchanged.
- Gate 1 is unchanged: it still compares the engine and negadoctor with NegPy only.
- Cost: on a synthetic 6000 by 4000 render against a 4600 by 3000 reference the registration takes about 0.1 s in release and the overlap statistics about 0.5 s (the pixel-wise path costs the same per pixel); the render itself is over a second. The JPEG reader is the `image` crate already in the workspace (negg-io uses it to write), now also a dependency of negg-cli. A tracked case whose NLP reference becomes unaligned is reported MISSING and fails the gate, which is the conservative verdict.
- Rotation (Ruling 108): every real export was turned 0.5 to 1.1 degrees against the render by hand straightening, and without a rotation parameter two of four real references failed to align (edge correlation 0.185 and 0.377, against 0.66 and 0.78 with it) and the aligned ones carried residual misregistration. The search gains an angle `angle_degrees`, clockwise, about the centre of the oriented reference (so `offset` keeps its meaning), limited to `MAX_ROTATION_DEGREES = 2`. `MIN_CORRELATION` stays 0.5: unrelated scenes score 0.07 to 0.22 with or without the rotation (a rotated unrelated reference is a test). The climb moves the angle by the step that moves the reference's far corner by one level pixel, halved with the other steps down to an eighth of a pixel on the finest level (about 0.01 degrees at 6000 pixels), so the recovered angle is good to well under the 0.1 degree the tests require. A turn of 1 or 2 degrees changes the L* correlation too little for a climb from zero to find, so each seed on the first climbed level also starts at -1.2 and +1.2 degrees (`ANGLE_SEEDS`). Scale steps now keep the reference centre fixed, so scale does not trade against the offsets. With a rotation the overlap is no longer a rectangle: `overlap_rect` is the largest rectangle about the reference centre inside the turned reference (the same fraction of both half extents, 1.7% shorter per side at 1 degree on a square), cut to the render, so every scored pixel samples inside the reference; with angle 0 it is exactly the old rectangle. `aligned_stats` is unchanged (it resamples through `to_reference`). The report's `alignment` record carries `angle_degrees` (Ruling 110); older reports without it read as 0. The coverage bounds (`MIN_OVERLAP`, `MIN_REFERENCE_INSIDE`) are checked on that inscribed rectangle, which shrinks with the angle: the reference's share inside it is about 0.95 at 1 degree and 0.90 at 2 degrees on a 3:2 frame, so a placement near the 0.75 bound can fail at an angle it would pass at zero. Controller choice, TODO(measure) on the limit, the seed angles and the corner step.
- Flat surfaces (Ruling 108): a dark, low-texture frame (a faint ramp that every placement of a crop matches in L*, plus a few thin edges) left the coarse L* surface a flat ridge, and the coarse search plus its hill climb settled about 6 pixels off at a scale of 0.96 (the synthetic dark-ramp test). Three changes, each needed: the best 3 distinct placements per orientation are kept from the exhaustive search (`COARSE_SEEDS`, distinct beyond 1.5 coarse pixels or 4% in scale) instead of one; they are not climbed on the coarsest level (that climb slides along the ridge) but start on the next level, where the field is cut to 6 and then 2 per level with climbs that ended on the same placement merged; and from a long side of 144 pixels (`EDGE_MIN_LONG_SIDE`) the objective is the mean of the L* and the gradient magnitude correlations, which is blind to the ramp. Gradient magnitude at the coarse levels was tried and rejected: blurred texture edges are noise there and pulled an ordinary textured frame 5 pixels off. The exhaustive search stays on L*. All of `COARSE_SEEDS`, the separations, `EDGE_MIN_LONG_SIDE`, the survivor counts and `ROTATION_MIN_LONG_SIDE` are Controller choice, TODO(measure). `MARGIN` (0.05), `MIN_OVERLAP` (0.3) and `MIN_REFERENCE_INSIDE` (0.75) are tagged the same way in the source.
- Cost with rotation: the 6000 by 4000 render against the 4600 by 3000 reference registers in about 0.12 s in release (0.08 to 0.09 s before; measured on a loaded machine), still far under the render time.
- Rules out: free rotation beyond 2 degrees, perspective and non-uniform scale between render and reference; references covering less than 60% or more than 110% of the render width.

## Eval delta

None, no render change. The committed corpus has no frames; the eval output on it is byte-identical to `eval-baseline.json`.
